Privacy Policy

OncologySCE · Last updated 30 September 2026

OncologySCE ("the app") is a study aid for the UK Medical Oncology SCE. This policy explains what data the app handles, where it goes and why. In short: the app has no user accounts, no advertising and no third party analytics or tracking SDKs. Your progress syncs to your own iCloud. A small amount of data reaches the developer: community answer statistics that do not name you, problem reports and emails you choose to send, the purchase check that unlocks the question bank, and, only if you opt in, research data.

Who is responsible

The data controller is Cian Murphy, the developer of OncologySCE, based in the United Kingdom. Contact: oncologysce@proton.me.

Your progress and history (your iCloud)

Your quiz progress (questions answered, scores, flagged questions, review schedule and session history) is stored on your device. If you are signed in to iCloud, it also syncs between your own devices through Apple's iCloud (iCloud key-value storage and your private CloudKit database). This copy lives in your iCloud account under Apple's privacy policy. The developer cannot see or access it. You can erase it with "Reset progress" in Settings, by deleting the app, or through your iCloud storage settings.

Community answer statistics

After you answer a question, the app can show what percentage of other users chose each option. To make this work, the first time you answer each question the app saves a small record to the app's CloudKit public database (run by Apple). Each record holds the question's ID, the option you chose, whether it was correct, and a random ID created by the app on your device (not your name, email or Apple ID). CloudKit also notes which iCloud account created the record, using an opaque identifier that Apple does not reveal as your name or Apple ID.

The app only ever displays these records as totals and percentages. Because the database is public to the app, other copies of the app can read the individual records in order to count them, but those records contain nothing that names you. The records are kept while the app runs this feature. They are not used to build a profile of you. If you want your records removed, email us.

Reporting a problem

If you tap "Report a problem" on a question, the app sends the note you type, the question's ID and topic, and the app version to the developer using Apple's CloudKit public database. CloudKit associates the report with your iCloud account so duplicates can be followed up, but we do not see your name, email or Apple ID from it. We use reports only to find and fix errors in the question bank. Please do not include personal or patient information in the note. Reporting is optional and the app works fully without it.

Unlocking the question bank (purchase check)

The full question bank is downloaded from our server, a Cloudflare Worker, over an encrypted (HTTPS) connection. Before it will send the bank, the app sends the Worker Apple's signed proof of purchase: the AppTransaction (showing this Apple Account got the app) and, for subscribers, the signed subscription transaction. The Worker checks Apple's signature and returns a short-lived access token, which the app stores in your device's Keychain. These proofs contain Apple's transaction identifiers, product and dates, but no name, email or Apple ID.

The Worker may log a transaction identifier briefly when a check fails, and it keeps a list of refunded or revoked transaction identifiers so that a refunded purchase cannot keep unlocking the bank. Apple also sends the Worker notices about subscription events (for example renewals and refunds), which record the product, dates, price and transaction identifiers. We use this only to run the subscription and prevent misuse. Payment itself is handled entirely by Apple.

Research data (optional)

You can choose to share your answers with the developer to help find out how scores in the app relate to real SCE results, and to improve the questions. This is off unless you turn it on. The app asks you when you first use it. If you close the question without answering, it asks again on a later launch, no more than three times in all on each device. If you share your answers, it may also ask you once, separately, whether you want to add your exam result. Saying no changes nothing about how the app works for you.

What is collected, if you opt in

  • For each answer: the question's ID, the option you chose (or that you left it blank in a mock), the answer that was keyed correct at the time, how long you took, and whether you marked it as unsure.
  • For each session: when it started and which topic or mode it covered.
  • Your past quiz history already on your device (or synced to it from your other devices), which is uploaded once when you opt in, then new sessions as you complete them.
  • The app version.
  • Only if you separately agree: your SCE result for a sitting (for example September 2026), given as pass, fail, did not sit or prefer not to say, and optionally your scaled score.
  • A record of each consent choice you make, with its date, so we can show what you agreed to.

What is not collected

Your name, email address, Apple ID, phone number, device identifiers, device model or location. Your IP address is not stored with research data (Cloudflare necessarily sees it to deliver any internet request, as described below).

Your research ID

When you opt in, the app creates a random research ID and a secret key. Your data is stored under that ID, not under your name. The ID and key are kept in your iCloud key-value storage so your own devices share one ID. You can see your research ID in Settings, Research sharing. Because answers and an exam result together could in principle relate to a real person, we treat this data as pseudonymous personal data and protect it accordingly.

Why, and on what legal basis

Purposes: to evaluate how performance in the app relates to SCE results, and to improve question quality (for example spotting questions that are unclear or wrongly keyed). The lawful basis is your consent (UK GDPR Article 6(1)(a)). Sharing answers and sharing an exam result are separate choices. You can withdraw either at any time in Settings, Research sharing, and withdrawing has no effect on your use of the app. Withdrawal stops any further uploads straight away. It does not affect processing that took place before you withdrew. After you withdraw, data you already shared is not used in any new analysis, and it is deleted when you choose "Delete my shared data" or when the retention period ends, whichever comes first.

Who can see it

Only the developer. It is not sold, not shared with other organisations, not used for advertising, and not used to make any automated decision about you. Any findings that are published or presented will be aggregate only (for example group averages over at least five people), and no individual will be identifiable from them.

Where it is stored

Research data is stored in a Cloudflare D1 database set to Cloudflare's EU jurisdiction, so it is stored in the European Union. Cloudflare acts as our processor under its Data Processing Addendum. Cloudflare is a US company and its global network carries the requests, so some processing may take place outside the UK and EU. Those transfers are covered by the safeguards in Cloudflare's Data Processing Addendum (Standard Contractual Clauses with the UK Addendum) and Cloudflare's certification under the UK Extension to the EU US Data Privacy Framework.

How long it is kept

Research data is deleted automatically 2 years after your research ID was last used (for example your last upload), or immediately when you delete it.

Your rights

  • Access and portability: Settings, Research sharing, "Download my data" gives you a copy of everything held under your research ID, in a machine readable file.
  • Erasure: "Delete my shared data" in the same screen deletes it from the server straight away.
  • Rectification, restriction and objection: email oncologysce@proton.me with your research ID (shown in the app). We cannot find your data from your name or email, because we do not hold them.
  • Complaints: you can complain to the UK Information Commissioner's Office at ico.org.uk. We would appreciate the chance to deal with your concern first.

We reply to requests within one month.

Network use

Requests to our Worker and question images are delivered by Cloudflare. As with any internet request, Cloudflare processes standard technical information such as your IP address to deliver it and to protect the service, and may keep short-lived technical logs. See Cloudflare's privacy policy. Our own code does not store your IP address.

The optional ChatGPT panel opens OpenAI's website inside the app. Anything you do there, including signing in, is between you and OpenAI under OpenAI's privacy policy. The app does not send your progress or research data to OpenAI.

Contacting us

The app offers an optional "Submit feedback" link that opens your email app. If you choose to email us, we will see your email address and whatever you write, and use it only to respond to you.

Children

The app is intended for medical professionals and trainees and is not directed at children.

Changes

If this policy changes, the updated version will be posted on this page with a new date. If a change affects research data, the app will ask for your consent again before any new use.
